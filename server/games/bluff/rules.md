# Bluff

Also known as **Cheat** or **I Doubt It**. A game of nerve: play your cards face down, say what they are, and hope nobody calls you out.

## Objective

Be the first player to get rid of all your cards.

## Players

3–8 players. With 6 or more players, the host can switch to **2 decks** in the lobby.

## Cards and ranking

A standard 52-card deck (or two). Suits do not matter. For claims, ranks are just names: A, 2, 3 … 10, J, Q, K.

## Setup / dealing

The whole deck is shuffled and dealt out one card at a time. Some players may get one more card than others — that's fine. The player holding the **A♠** starts.

## How a turn works

There are two claim modes, chosen by the host.

### Round leader picks the rank (default)

1. **Leading a round:** the leader puts 1–4 cards face down on the pile and claims a rank, e.g. *"three Kings"*. The cards don't have to be Kings.
2. **Following:** going clockwise, each player either
   - plays 1–4 cards face down claiming **the same rank** ("two more Kings"), or
   - **passes**.
3. **Calling bluff:** after any play, *any* other player may press **Call bluff!** — you don't need to wait for your turn. The play can be challenged until the next player acts.
   - The last play is turned face up.
   - If **any** of those cards is not the claimed rank, the player who played them was bluffing and **picks up the entire pile**.
   - If they were **all** the claimed rank, the caller was wrong and **picks up the entire pile**.
   - Whoever *won* the challenge starts a new round with any rank.
4. **Everyone passes:** if every other player passes after a play, the round ends. The pile is set aside (out of the game) and the last player who played starts a new round. *(The host can turn "set pile aside" off, so the pile stays and keeps growing.)*

### Ranks go in order (classic Cheat)

Claims must follow the sequence **A, 2, 3 … 10, J, Q, K, A …** — each player claims the next rank in turn and must play at least one card (no passing). Calling bluff works exactly as above, and the challenge winner plays next with the next rank in the sequence.

You may play at most **4 cards** at once (8 with two decks).

## Winning

When you play your last card(s), you have not won yet — the other players can still call bluff on that play.

- If someone calls and you were **telling the truth**, you win immediately.
- If someone calls and you were **bluffing**, you pick up the pile and play on.
- If nobody calls before the next player plays or passes, your play stands and **you win**.

## Scoring

There are no points: the first player out of cards wins. Everyone else is ranked by how many cards they still hold (fewest first).

## Example turns

> **Asha** leads and plays 2 cards face down: *"two Sevens."* (She actually played a 7 and a Queen.)
> **Ben** has no Sevens. He plays one card anyway: *"one Seven."*
> **Chen** is holding three Sevens, so she knows Ben can't have more than one… and Asha's two look suspicious too, but only the **last** play can be challenged. She presses **Call bluff!** on Ben.
> Ben's card is turned up: a 4. Ben was bluffing, so he picks up all 3 cards in the pile. Chen won the challenge and leads the next round.

> **Dev** has one card left. He plays it: *"one Ace."* Nobody calls bluff, and **Eli**, the next player, passes. Dev's play stands and he wins the game.

## Tips

- Claim ranks you hold several of — the truth is your best cover.
- Counting matters: if you hold three Kings, someone claiming two Kings is lying.
- Calling bluff costs you the pile if you're wrong, so pick your moments.
