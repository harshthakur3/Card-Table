# Oh Hell

Also called **Contract Whist**, **Up and Down the River** or **Blackout**. Every round you predict exactly how many tricks you will win — one too many is as bad as one too few.

## Objective

Score the most points by bidding **exactly** the number of tricks you will take, round after round.

## Players

3–7 players, one 52-card deck.

## Card ranking

Within each suit: **A (high) K Q J 10 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 (low)**. A trump beats any card of another suit.

## Setup / dealing

- The game has several rounds. By default the hand size goes **up then down**: 1 card, 2 cards … up to the maximum, then back down to 1. The host can pick the maximum (it's lowered automatically so everyone gets that many cards with one card left over) and the direction.
- The dealer rotates one seat to the left each round.
- After dealing, the next card is **turned face up**: its suit is **trumps** for the round.

## How a round works

### 1. Bidding

Starting left of the dealer and going clockwise, each player bids how many tricks they expect to win (0 up to the hand size). Bids are public.

**Hook rule** (on by default): the dealer bids last and may **not** bid a number that makes the total of all bids equal to the number of tricks. That way somebody always misses.

### 2. Playing tricks

- The player left of the dealer leads the first trick with any card.
- Everyone else must **follow suit** if they can. If they can't, they may play any card, including a trump.
- The highest trump wins the trick; if no trump was played, the highest card of the suit that was led wins.
- The winner of a trick leads the next one.

### 3. Scoring

| Mode | Exact bid | Missed bid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Classic** (default) | 10 + bid | 0 |
| 1 per trick | 1 per trick won + 10 bonus | 1 per trick won |
| Penalty | 10 + bid | −1 per trick off |

A bid of 0 that you make exactly is worth 10 in classic scoring — zero bids are powerful!

## Winning

After the last round, the player with the most points wins. Ties share the win.

## Example turns

> Round 4, 4 cards each, the turned-up card is **7♥** so hearts are trumps.
> Bids: Asha 1, Ben 2, Chen 0. Dev is the dealer and bids last. 1 + 2 + 0 = 3, so Dev **can't** bid 1 (that would make 4 = the number of tricks). Dev bids 2.
> Asha leads **K♠**. Ben follows with **3♠**. Chen has no spades and plays **2♥** — a trump! Dev plays **A♠**. Chen's tiny trump wins the trick, which is bad news for her zero bid.

> At the end of the round Asha took 1 trick (bid 1): **+11**. Ben took 2 (bid 2): **+12**. Chen took 1 (bid 0): **0**. Dev took 0 (bid 2): **0**.

## Tips

- High trumps and aces are your sure tricks; everything else is a guess.
- As the hands shrink, a bid of 0 becomes both tempting and dangerous.
- Watch the total bids: if everyone overbid, expect players to fight for tricks.
